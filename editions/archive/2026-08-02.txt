THE CULTURE FILE

The Blue Ridge Parkway — 469 miles connecting Shenandoah to Great Smoky Mountains — was the last Depression-era public works project, completed in 1987 after 52 years of construction. It remains the most-visited unit of the National Park Service, with no entrance fee and no speed limit higher than 45 mph. Craggy Gardens, your golden-hour stop, sits atop a massive quartz monzonite dome that geologists estimate is 400 million years old — older than the Atlantic Ocean, older than bones. The parkway was designed by landscape architect Stanley Abbott, who insisted that every curve follow the natural contour of the ridge and that no commercial signage ever appear along its length. It worked: you drive 469 miles and never see a billboard, a gas station, or a traffic light.
