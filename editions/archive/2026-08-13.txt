TRIVIA THURSDAY

Newport, Rhode Island: the gilded summer colony that hosted America's most extravagant parties and still holds the record for America's Cup defenses. The Breakers — Cornelius Vanderbilt II's 70-room "cottage" — took two years and 2,000 workers to build, opening in 1895. The Great Hall rises 50 feet. The dining room seats 34 under a ceiling painted to look like a Renaissance sky. Every doorknob, every hinge, every switchplate was custom-made. It cost $12 million then — about $400 million in today's money — for a house the family used ten weeks a year.

Here's your question: The Breakers replaced an earlier mansion on the same site that burned down in 1892. What was that first house called?

THE CULTURE FILE

Newport's Cliff Walk — the 3.5-mile public footpath behind the mansions — exists because Rhode Island law has guaranteed shore access since colonial times. The Gilded Age families tried to close it. They lost. It's been a National Recreation Trail since 1975. You'll walk the Forty Steps section on the 21st: carved stone stairs dropping to the rocks, built in 1845 for the servants of the big houses to reach the water after dark. The Irish maids who worked the cottages would gather there on summer nights to dance.

Tennis at the International Tennis Hall of Fame (Newport's Casino, built 1880) still happens on grass courts. The US Open was born there in 1881.

Vanderbilt money also built The Wayfinder, your Newport inn — formerly Vanderbilt Grace, renovated 2024. Same harborfront block, very different century.
