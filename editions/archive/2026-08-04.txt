TASTE TUESDAY

Boatyard Bar & Grill sits at the working end of Annapolis — Eastport, across Spa Creek from the postcard docks. It's a locals' spot that happens to serve one of the Chesapeake's great crab feasts: steamed blues by the dozen, dumped on brown paper, mallets and Old Bay within reach. The vibe is summer-camp picnic table, the crabs are ridiculously fresh, and the Bay Bridge lights shimmer across the water at dusk. You're going on a Tuesday night — the quiet side of the week, when the waitstaff remembers your name by dessert. This is the Maryland claim anchor, and it tastes exactly like the state it represents.

THE CULTURE FILE

Monticello appears on the nickel's reverse — the only private residence ever honored on circulating U.S. currency. Jefferson designed every window, every column, the hidden staircases, the skylights, the revolving serving door between dining room and kitchen. He planted 330 varieties of vegetables in the thousand-foot terraced garden and kept meteorological records for fifty years. The house took forty years to finish — he was still redesigning rooms the year he died there, July 4, 1826, exactly fifty years after the Declaration. John Adams died the same day, five hours later, 500 miles north. Adams's last words: "Thomas Jefferson survives." He was wrong by five hours.
