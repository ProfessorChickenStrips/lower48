THE CULTURE FILE

Portland, Maine has been a working waterport since 1633, but its literary heart belongs to Henry Wadsworth Longfellow, who was born here in 1807. His childhood home still stands on Congress Street — now a museum — and the man who wrote "I stood on the bridge at midnight" and "Listen, my children, and you shall hear" spent his formative years watching schooners work Casco Bay. The city's Old Port district, where you'll spend your final two evenings, was rebuilt in brick and cast iron after the Great Fire of 1866 — a blaze that started on the Fourth of July and consumed 1,800 buildings in ten hours. What stands now is a Victorian phoenix: cobblestone streets, gas lamps, the densest concentration of 19th-century commercial architecture in America, and forty-seven restaurants within six blocks.

"I have an affection for a great city. I feel safe in the neighborhood of man, and enjoy the sweet security of the streets." — Longfellow, from *Hyperion*
