THE CULTURE FILE

The Blue Ridge Parkway — all 469 miles of it — was the most ambitious Depression-era public works project that wasn't a dam. It took twenty years to finish (1935–1955) and employed thousands. The route was designed not for speed but for scenery: every curve, every overlook, every tunnel was placed to frame the view. No billboards. No commercial traffic. Just the mountains and the road. When you drive it on Day Two, you're driving a work of art that happens to be asphalt.

Craggy Gardens, your sunset stop, sits at 5,500 feet. In June the rhododendrons bloom so thick the mountainside looks like it's on fire. In August you get the haze instead — that soft blue fog that gave the range its name — and the light at the end of the day turns the whole ridge gold.
