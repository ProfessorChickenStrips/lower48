Subject: The Morning Mile - 14 days

Two weeks from this morning, you'll wake up in Asheville with every state still waiting to be claimed.

STAY SATURDAY

The Blind Tiger rises from the bones of the Church of St. Lawrence — Asheville's first Catholic parish, built 1909, decommissioned 1977, rescued from demolition by a determined preservationist in 2012. The conversion kept the sanctuary's soaring ceiling, the original stained glass, and the confessional booths (now wine storage). Your room sits where the choir once stood. One Michelin Key, earned honestly: the morning spread arrives on three-tiered stands, the rooftop overlooks the Blue Ridge foothills, and every turn reveals some fragment of the building's first life held in careful tension with its second.

The inn anchors your first two nights — arrival evening August 15th, then the full Biltmore day on the 16th. You're four blocks from the South Slope breweries, six from Pack Square, eight from Cúrate if you book it. The Blind Tiger holds the strange honor of being both your opening bell and your last chance to sleep past sunrise until Portland, twelve days and nine states later.

Rafael Guastavino, the Catalan architect who built the Biltmore's vaulted ceilings, also designed St. Lawrence Basilica six blocks north — the largest freestanding elliptical dome in North America, all self-supporting tile, no beams. He's buried in its crypt. Asheville was his American workshop; the city still carries his fingerprints.

One confession: the Blind Tiger's $200 Sunday premium made the math impossible, so you're eastbound Sunday morning instead of Monday. The upside? You gain a full touring day in Charlottesville and dodge the Biltmore's weekend crowds. The cost? Monday's leisurely UVA afternoon becomes Tuesday's Monticello morning with a Beltway crossing deadline — but you booked the earliest tour, so it's clean.
