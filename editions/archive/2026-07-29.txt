WANDER WEDNESDAY

The Kancamagus Highway — 34.5 miles of New Hampshire Route 112 that never once meets a gas station, a traffic light, or a boardwalk selling saltwater taffy — is the White Mountains' signature drive, and it earns the reputation honestly. The road was completed in 1959 and named for a 17th-century Pennacook chief whose name translates to "the fearless one." It peaks at 2,855 feet at Kancamagus Pass, threading through stands of birch and sugar maple that make it one of New England's most famous foliage routes come October. In August, the reward is different: the trees are full and green, the overlooks are yours, and the handful of trails that spur off the highway — Sabbaday Falls, Rocky Gorge, Lower Falls — deliver cold-water swimming holes and moss-covered granite you'll have largely to yourselves on a weekday morning. The Sunday edition will map the route in full, but the iron rule stands now: fuel the car the night before in Jackson. There is no gas between Conway and Lincoln. The highway does not forgive optimism.

THE CULTURE FILE

The White Mountains have drawn artists since the 1820s, when Thomas Cole and the Hudson River School painters arrived to capture the wild grandeur of Crawford Notch and Mount Washington's summit. Their canvases helped spark America's first tourism boom and established the Whites as a Romantic pilgrimage site. A century later, Robert Frost spent summers in Franconia — just west of your route — and wrote "Stopping by Woods on a Snowy Evening" about a winter drive through these same forests. The region's most famous architectural contribution is the grand resort hotel: The Mount Washington Hotel in Bretton Woods (visible from Route 302) hosted the 1944 Bretton Woods Conference, which established the World Bank and the International Monetary Fund. You'll pass its white colonial facade on the drive through Crawford Notch.
