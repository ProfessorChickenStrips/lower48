WANDER WEDNESDAY — The Blue Ridge Parkway

America's favorite drive runs 469 miles from Shenandoah to the Smokies, but you'll claim the best 50: Asheville north through Craggy Gardens to the Virginia line. It's the section that proves the axiom — the Parkway was built for 45 mph because 45 mph is the speed at which Appalachia unfolds correctly. Three facts the postcards don't mention: the roadway has zero commercial traffic (it's federally prohibited), which means no semis, no billboards, just the ridgeline and you; Craggy Gardens' rhododendron tunnels bloom late May through mid-June, but the golden-hour light in mid-August turns the balds into something just as good; and the overlooks were surveyed during the Depression by crews who walked every mile on foot before a single shovel touched dirt. They were choosing views for a country that needed proof the future still held something worth seeing.

Your day-two plan runs Biltmore first, then Antler Hill's winery tasting, then the Parkway north to Craggy Gardens for sunset — arriving around 7:30 PM when the light drops gold across the peaks. That's the shot. That's the one that goes on the wall.

THE CULTURE FILE — The Biltmore's quiet Hollywood career

George Vanderbilt's 250-room château has stood in for itself and others in more than a dozen films. *Being There* (1979) used the estate's grounds for the Rand mansion; *The Swan* (1956) filmed ballroom scenes in the house; *Richie Rich* (1994) turned it into the Rich family's absurd home; and *Last of the Mohicans* (1992) shot surrounding forest scenes in the estate's back acreage. The house also appeared in *Forrest Gump* — not as a location, but in a visual effects composite. When Biltmore opened for its first public tours in 1930 (five years after George's death, during the Depression), the family did it to keep the estate operational and the staff employed. Turns out 11,000 acres and a French Renaissance château cost a lot to heat.
