Subject: The Morning Mile - Day 4 of 14: Monticello & Annapolis

Good morning from Charlottesville — you're waking at the Oakhurst Inn with the whole day still ahead of you.

YESTERDAY, IN YOUR OWN WORDS

"Not a lot of pictures today but we did eat our best meal yet!" — Maggie

TODAY'S BRIEFING

Monticello Behind the Scenes tour, 9:00 AM — booked and locked. The full-house tour: upstairs rooms + the Dome Room that standard visits never see. Done by 11:00, on the road by noon. Then 120 miles in about two and a quarter hours over the Bay Bridge. Light drizzle forecast for Annapolis with a high near 75 and gentle wind — soft weather for a walking afternoon at the Naval Academy and City Dock. One traffic rule: the Bay Bridge backs up brutally on summer Fridays; Tuesdays run clean.

THE CLAIM OF THE DAY

Virginia — the tenth colony, the first state. Your anchor is Thomas Jefferson's own mountaintop: the house he spent forty years designing, the dome he climbed a ladder to perfect, the rooms he filled with the whole Enlightenment. The ritual: one local meal (crab feast tonight counts), one photo of the two of you together, and the stamp in the Ledger before bed. The stamp is the trophy — unstamped days haunt.

WEATHER & THE RAIN FALLBACK

Annapolis expects light drizzle this afternoon: 75 degrees, barely a breeze. The Academy and the Dock both work beautifully wet — you're already dressed for it, and Ego Alley under gray skies has its own charm. If the rain turns serious, duck into the Maryland State House (oldest state capitol in continuous use, where Washington resigned his commission) or the Banneker-Douglass Museum. Dinner is indoors either way.

TONIGHT

Graduate Annapolis. Dinner: Boatyard Bar & Grill in Eastport — the crab feast that anchors the claim. If you booked, you're set; if not, they take walk-ins and the wait is part of the ritual. Before lights out: one sentence in the journal. Tomorrow's edition reads it, and so does forever.

POSTCARD

"I cannot live without books." — Thomas Jefferson, 1815, in a letter to John Adams after selling his library to rebuild the Library of Congress following the British burning of Washington.

ONE DELIGHT TO LOOK FOR

At Monticello, the Great Clock above the entrance hall: Jefferson designed it to run on cannonball weights descending through holes he cut in the floor. He miscalculated — Saturday's weight disappears into the basement.

P.S. — Forty-three of fifty and counting. Two more states fall today.
