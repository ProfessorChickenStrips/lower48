THE CULTURE FILE

Asheville's art deco downtown survived urban renewal because the Great Depression froze development for 50 years — the 1920s buildings stayed untouched into the preservation era. Thomas Wolfe wrote Look Homeward, Angel about this town (his childhood home is now a museum). F. Scott Fitzgerald stayed at the Grove Park Inn in 1935 and 1936, working through what would become The Crack-Up essays. And the banjo — the instrument most people associate with Appalachia — came here by way of enslaved West Africans in the 18th century; its modern bluegrass sound was born in these mountains.

ONE DELIGHT TO LOOK FOR

The Blind Tiger itself: it's a Queen Anne Victorian from 1896, named for the Prohibition-era speakeasies that hid behind false storefronts. This one hid nothing — it was a boarding house, then a private home, then silent for years before its current life as an inn. Ask about the original pocket doors.
